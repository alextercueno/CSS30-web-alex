Part 02 — Toward a solution

What clearing the water actually takes

Fixing this doesn't require reinventing anything — it requires finishing what's already been started. Three conrete, sequential fixes at the sewage level, backed by frameworks already in motion since 2014.

STEP 01
Clear the open canals

Remove debris, dirt, and trash from the sewer canals first. This alone restores the wastewate flow and cuts the odor that currently forces pedestrians to swerve away from sidewalks nationwide.

STEP 02
Close the canals

Covering open canals reduces contamination exposure and foul odor at the source, while reclaiming usable sidewalk space in dense urban areas.

STEP 03
Treat the wastewater

Proper, timely wastewater treatment — not just containment — is what ultimately makes the water safe again for the communities living closest to it.

Since 2014
Sanitation Safety Planning (SSP)

WHO, the Asian Development Bank, and the Philippine DOH use SSP as a household-level risk assessment tool — a direct stepping stone toward the 2030 targets for reduced water-pollution deaths and water sanitation.

Target: 2020
National Sewerage & Septage Management Program

The Philippine government set a water-quality goal through NSSMP for 2020. As of 2025, no noticeable improvement to the sewage system has followed — the gap between policy and pavement.

Ongoing
Private vs. Public maintenance

Visible maintenance along Manila's streets is largely private water suppliers serving paying customers — not government-opereated repair of the public sewage system that everyone depends on.

Every organization willing to help moves this forward.

Governments have set targets before and missed them. What's closed the gap so far has largely been individual donations, awareness efforts, and organizations working without expecting anything in return. That's where support — and funding — has the clearest path to impact.
